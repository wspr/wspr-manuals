\section{Introduction}

This manual includes everything you need to know about running a research project with Will.

\section{Comms and Files}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Will will create a MS Teams channel for the project.

\item Use Teams for all non-critical comms. Remember if you do not @-mention either the channel or an individual, there is a high chance that the message will go unnoticed (academic staff work with many tens of Teams channels).

\item For critical emails, use Teams \emph{but also} send an email (e.g., `here is our report draft, final version due in 12 days. Please provide feedback by the end of the week...').

\item Use OneDrive to sync the files from this channel to your Mac/PC and use it as the working folder and archive for the project.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Software}

\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item CAD --- please use the browser-based Onshape software. Will will add you to his team there so you can browse and re-use designs.
\item Reports --- please use Overleaf and write your documents in LaTeX. Although perhaps a learning curve for some of you, it will pay dividends. Will can help solve almost any LaTeX problem and is happy to help.
\item Git and Github --- if you are writing (substantial) code, it is best to do this using a Github repository. Will will set you up.
\end{enumerate}

\section{AI}

\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item Use of AI tools is not forbidden. But some thought is needed to use them appropriately and well.

\item AI can be used like Wikipedia --- a good way to get started finding key references (or at least semi-appropriate) references for the topics of interest for
your project. AI does not obviate the requirement to read those references and form your own synthesis!

\item AI is great at setting up back-of-the-envelope calculations. Be \emph{very} careful of these; all assumptions need independent verification and all outputs need further validation.

\item The two points above mean that all projects should now have no excuse for not progressing when faced with a knowledge gap. AI is a highly useful tool for getting started and maintaining momentum.

\item AI is also excellent --- perhaps too good --- at technical writing. This is a trap; use it to check your work only, but never copy/paste AI text into your report. This is plagiarism.

\item Due to the point above, there should be no excuse for sloppy writing. It is easier and easier to get your writing to a competent standard; you just need the time to iterate your drafts at least once.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Group work}

\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item It is the responsibility of all group members to ensure the whole team is productively contributing. Peer assessment scaling is intended to indicate differences, not to fundamentally fix any mismatches in output/reward.
\item Corollary: when groups fall apart, they should be split to allow each subgroup to be assessed on their own merits.
\item Students should expect to have their own independent lines of investigations and responsibilities in the project. Approximately one objective and one report chapter per student should be planned.
\item Individual responsilities does not mean solo work. Multiple group members may contribute to an objective (often it works well to pair up), but one member remains `responsible' for the success of that objective.
\item Corollary: plan to have $N$ or $N+1$ technical objectives, and ensure the Gantt chart reflects enough detail to show that everyone has something to do in all phases of the project.
\end{enumerate}
